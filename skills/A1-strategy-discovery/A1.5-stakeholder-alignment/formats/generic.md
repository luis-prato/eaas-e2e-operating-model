# A1.5 — Stakeholder Alignment

> **EaaS E2E Operating Model** · Group: A1 · Strategy & Discovery · Role: Account Executive
> Version: 1.24.0 · License: Apache-2.0 · Author: Luis Prato

**Summary:** Map the customer decision-making unit, understand each stakeholder's priorities, and build the internal coalition needed to approve and sustain an EaaS contract.

**Key Outcomes:**
- Stakeholder map and influence analysis
- Champion identification and engagement plan
- Objection register with response playbook
- Internal approval pathway documentation

---

## What This Skill Covers

Stakeholder Alignment is the process of identifying, mapping, and building consensus among all parties who influence or are affected by an EaaS contract — on both the customer and provider sides. It ensures that no critical stakeholder is discovered late in the process, derailing an otherwise sound deal.

---

## Why It Matters in EaaS

EaaS contracts involve a broader stakeholder set than traditional equipment sales. Operations, Finance, Legal, Procurement, IT, Maintenance, and often the CFO all have roles. A deal that wins over Operations but fails at Procurement — or is approved by the plant manager but blocked by corporate treasury — is a deal lost. Stakeholder alignment is deal insurance.

---

## Stakeholder Mapping Framework

### Customer-Side Stakeholders

| Role | Primary Concern | Influence Level | EaaS-Specific Risk |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Operations Manager | Uptime, productivity | High | Sceptical of outcome promises |
| CFO / Finance | Cash flow, balance sheet | High | Concerned about OpEx vs. CapEx classification |
| Procurement | Vendor terms, compliance | Medium | Wants standard terms; resistant to novel contracts |
| Legal / Risk | Contract risk, liability | Medium–High | Unfamiliar with outcome-based contract language |
| IT / OT | Data security, connectivity | Medium | Concerned about remote access and data ownership |
| Maintenance | Job security, process change | Low–Medium | May resist predictive maintenance changing their role |
| Plant Manager / CEO | Strategic outcomes | High | Key sponsor; must be kept engaged |

### Provider-Side Stakeholders

| Role | Concern | Alignment Need |
|------|---------|----------------|
| Sales | Win the deal | Align on realistic commitments |
| Finance / Pricing | Margin protection | Validate commercial model |
| Service / Operations | Delivery capability | Confirm service scope is deliverable |
| Legal | Contract risk | Review non-standard terms |
| Technology / Engineering | Technical feasibility | Validate outcome commitments |

---

## Key Activities

1. **Stakeholder identification workshop** — Run a structured session to list all affected parties on both sides
2. **Influence / Interest mapping** — Place each stakeholder on a 2×2 grid (influence vs. interest) to prioritise engagement
3. **Objection pre-emption** — For each key stakeholder, identify likely objections and prepare responses
4. **Internal alignment meeting** — Align provider team on deal strategy, commitments, and negotiation red lines
5. **Customer executive briefing** — Present the EaaS concept at C-level to secure senior sponsorship before detailed design
6. **Ongoing check-ins** — Schedule regular touchpoints to maintain alignment as the deal evolves

---

## Key Outputs

- Stakeholder Map (visual and tabular)
- Engagement Plan (who to involve, when, with what message)
- Objection Register with prepared responses
- Internal Deal Strategy document
