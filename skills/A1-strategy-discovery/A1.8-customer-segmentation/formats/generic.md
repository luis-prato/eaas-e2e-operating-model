# A1.8 — Customer Segmentation

> **EaaS E2E Operating Model** · Group: A1 · Strategy & Discovery · Role: Commercial Director
> Version: 1.24.0 · License: Apache-2.0 · Author: Luis Prato

**Summary:** Identify which customer segments are the best candidates for EaaS — based on operational maturity, contract appetite, and outcome alignment — to focus sales effort where conversion is highest.

**Key Outcomes:**
- EaaS customer segmentation model
- Ideal Customer Profile for EaaS
- Segment-specific value proposition
- Target account list and prioritisation

---

## What This Skill Covers

Customer Segmentation identifies and profiles the types of customers that are most likely to succeed with EaaS — and most likely to generate sustainable returns for the provider. It moves beyond traditional market segmentation to score customers on EaaS-specific fit criteria.

---

## Why It Matters in EaaS

Not every customer who wants EaaS is a good EaaS customer. A customer with poor data infrastructure, adversarial procurement culture, or highly variable usage patterns creates disproportionate risk and operational cost for the provider. Segmentation protects margin and relationship quality.

---

## EaaS Customer Segmentation Criteria

### Tier 1: EaaS-Ready Customers (priority targets)

- Asset-intensive operations where equipment drives >30% of production cost
- Strong historical performance data (>2 years of uptime, maintenance records)
- Long-term strategic relationship orientation (vs. purely transactional procurement)
- IT/OT infrastructure that supports remote connectivity
- Demonstrated willingness to share operational data
- Finance team familiar with OpEx / operating lease concepts
- ≥5-year planning horizon for the specific asset category

### Tier 2: EaaS-Potential Customers (development targets)

- Asset-intensive but with data gaps that can be addressed in a 6–12 month readiness phase
- Transitioning from CapEx to OpEx mindset (often driven by CFO or PE pressure)
- First EaaS experience — may need more education and simpler initial contracts

### Tier 3: EaaS-Incompatible (avoid or defer)

- Short-term project-based usage (<3 years)
- Highly unpredictable asset usage (seasonal, event-driven)
- No connectivity infrastructure; unwilling to invest
- Adversarial procurement culture; focus purely on unit price
- Declining business with uncertain continuity

---

## Segmentation Variables for Industrial EaaS

| Variable | EaaS-Positive Indicator | EaaS-Negative Indicator |
|----------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Industry | Manufacturing, food processing, pharma, logistics | Construction, events, short-cycle projects |
| Asset criticality | Production-critical (stopping = loss of revenue) | Non-critical, backup equipment |
| Maintenance maturity | Structured CMMS in use | Reactive maintenance only |
| Financial profile | Stable, investment-grade; OpEx budget available | Stressed balance sheet; CapEx-only buyer |
| Relationship history | Existing supplier relationship | First-time interaction |
| Geography | Operates in markets where EaaS is legally feasible | Regulatory uncertainty around asset ownership |

---

## Key Activities

1. **Define segmentation criteria** — Agree the 5–7 criteria most predictive of EaaS success in your market
2. **Score existing pipeline** — Apply criteria to current opportunities; re-prioritise accordingly
3. **Build Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)** — Document the profile of the best EaaS customer in detail
4. **Design outreach strategy** — Tailor marketing and sales messages to each segment
5. **Review and update** — Revisit segmentation annually as EaaS market matures
