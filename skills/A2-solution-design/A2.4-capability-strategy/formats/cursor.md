**Group:** A2 · Solution Design

---

## What This Skill Covers

Capability Strategy identifies the organisational capabilities the provider needs to deliver the EaaS solution successfully — and designs a plan to build, buy, or partner for capabilities that are currently missing. It is the internal readiness assessment that mirrors the customer-facing solution design.

---

## Why It Matters in EaaS

Providers routinely win EaaS contracts based on the capabilities they aspire to have, not the ones they actually have. When delivery begins, capability gaps become visible — and expensive. Capability Strategy forces an honest internal assessment before contract commitments are made.

---

## EaaS Capability Framework

### Core EaaS Capability Domains

| Domain | Key Capabilities | Build / Buy / Partner? |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Commercial** | Outcome-based pricing, risk modelling, contract design | Typically build + legal partner |
| **Technology** | IoT platform, remote monitoring, data analytics, automated billing | Often SaaS + system integrator |
| **Service Operations** | Predictive maintenance, MTTR management, spare parts optimisation, field service scheduling | Build (core differentiator) |
| **Finance** | EaaS financial modelling, off-balance-sheet structuring, working capital management | Build + leasing partner |
| **Customer Success** | Performance reviews, relationship management, outcome reporting | Build |
| **Data & Analytics** | KPI measurement, SLA verification, energy monitoring, ESG reporting | Often platform + data team |

---

## Capability Assessment Scoring

For each required capability, assess:

- **Current Level** (1–5): 1 = absent, 5 = best-in-class
- **Required Level** (1–5): minimum needed to deliver contractual commitments
- **Gap** = Required − Current
- **Priority** = High / Medium / Low based on gap and capability criticality

---

## Capability Development Options

| Option | When to Use | Risk | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|------|----------|
| **Build internally** | Core differentiator; capability is long-term competitive advantage | Slower; requires talent investment | 12–36 months |
| **Buy** (M&A, team hire) | Capability needed quickly; proven team exists in market | Integration risk; cost | 6–18 months |
| **Partner / subcontract** | Non-core capability; partner already excels | Dependency risk; margin sharing | 1–6 months |
| **Platform / SaaS** | Technology capability; commoditised function | Vendor lock-in; ongoing cost | 1–3 months |

---

## Key Activities

1. **Capability inventory** — List all capabilities required to deliver the designed EaaS service
2. **Gap assessment** — Score current vs. required level for each capability
3. **Priority ranking** — Rank gaps by criticality to contract delivery
4. **Development planning** — For each critical gap: choose build/buy/partner; assign owner; set timeline
5. **Risk mitigation** — For capabilities that cannot be closed before contract start, design contractual protections or service scope limitations

---

## Key Outputs

- EaaS Capability Map (current state vs. required state)
- Capability Gap Analysis (prioritised)
- Build vs. Buy vs. Partner Decision Matrix
- Capability Development Roadmap (with owners, milestones, budgets)
- Contract Risk Register (capability-related risks)
