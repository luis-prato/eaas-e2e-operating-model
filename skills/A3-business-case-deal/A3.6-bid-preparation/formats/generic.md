## What This Skill Covers

Bid Preparation covers the detailed production of an EaaS proposal — from content planning through writing, design, review, and final submission. It translates the Win Strategy (from A3.3 Tendering Procedure) into a compelling, technically rigorous, commercially credible document.

---

## Why It Matters in EaaS

Winning EaaS tenders requires more than a good price. Evaluators need to believe the provider can deliver outcomes reliably over years. That belief is built through the quality, clarity, and specificity of the proposal — not just the numbers. A poorly prepared bid signals delivery risk before the contract has been signed.

---

## Bid Production Process

### Stage 1: Content Architecture (Days 1–3)
- Translate the Win Strategy into a proposal outline
- Assign sections to subject matter experts (technical, commercial, legal, ops)
- Identify evidence requirements: case studies, performance data, certifications
- Set internal review milestones and submission deadline countdown

### Stage 2: First Draft (Days 3–10)
- Write all proposal sections in parallel (see EaaS Proposal Structure in A3.3)
- Build the commercial model: pricing, payment schedule, indexation
- Design the SLA section with KPI definitions, measurement methodology, payment logic
- Source and format reference contracts and performance evidence

### Stage 3: Internal Review (Days 10–14)
- Commercial review: Is the pricing defensible? Is the margin acceptable?
- Legal review: Are there commitments we cannot contractually back?
- Operations review: Can we actually deliver what the proposal promises?
- Leadership sign-off: Is the bid approved for submission?

### Stage 4: Final Assembly & Submission (Days 14–Submit)
- Format and brand the proposal to customer requirements
- Cross-check compliance against all mandatory requirements in the RFP
- Complete all forms, certificates, and annexes
- Submit on time; confirm receipt in writing

## Proposal Quality Checklist

### Executive Summary
- [ ] States the outcome we guarantee in the first paragraph
- [ ] Explains why we are uniquely positioned to deliver it
- [ ] Is written for a non-technical decision-maker
- [ ] Is no longer than 2 pages

### Technical Solution
- [ ] Equipment specification matches the customer's stated requirements
- [ ] Performance data from comparable deployments is cited
- [ ] Technology platform (IoT, monitoring) is described specifically, not generically

### SLA Design
- [ ] KPIs are defined precisely (measurement formula included)
- [ ] Measurement period and start date are stated
- [ ] Payment structure and remedy mechanism are clear and fair
- [ ] Carve-outs for customer-caused events are included

### Commercial Offer
- [ ] All fees are itemised and explained
- [ ] Indexation mechanism is defined
- [ ] Payment terms are realistic and consistent with cash flow model
- [ ] Assumptions are stated so they can be verified

### Risk Management
- [ ] Key risks are disclosed and addressed — not hidden
- [ ] Force majeure clause is included
- [ ] Insurance coverage is specified

---

## Common Bid-Losing Mistakes

- Generic proposal language not tailored to the specific customer
- SLA promises that contradict the technical or operational capacity
- Missing mandatory forms or certifications (automatic disqualification)
- Pricing that cannot withstand a should-cost analysis by the customer
- Vague outcome statements ("high reliability") with no measurable definition
- Submitting at the last minute with no buffer for technical issues
